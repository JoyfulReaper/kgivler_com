My experience with GreenCloud

I currently use GreenCloud VPS for multiple public Linux VPS nodes, including Clanker and ScopeCreep.

These are not idle test machines. I use them for Docker services, monitoring, web applications, DN42 routing, WireGuard, DNS, small Internet-protocol servers, and networking experiments.

GreenCloud has been a good fit for the kind of inexpensive Linux infrastructure I run: enough resources to do useful work, cheap enough that keeping multiple locations online is practical, and support that has responded quickly when I have hit provider-side network problems.

Support experience

I have had a couple of network problems worth mentioning. I think those incidents are more useful in a review than pretending nothing has ever gone wrong, because they show what support does when the infrastructure is actually broken.

August 2026

IPv6 provisioning issue

After enabling IPv6 on one VPS, Neighbor Discovery to the supplied gateway worked and outbound packets left the VM, but nothing came back. Packet captures indicated that the problem was upstream of the guest.

I opened a ticket at 07:30. Support replied at 07:32, investigated the VPS, and at 07:48 asked me to retry after making a change. I confirmed IPv6 was working at 07:54.

About 24 minutes from opening the ticket to confirmed working IPv6.

September 2026

Unsolicited IPv6 multicast traffic

On another VPS I noticed inbound bandwidth increasing even though my applications were not receiving that amount of traffic.

A packet capture showed unsolicited ICMPv6 / MLDv2 multicast listener reports from another device on the provider-side network. During a 300-second capture I measured roughly 72,007 frames, about 107 MB, or approximately 2.85 Mbps of sustained inbound traffic from that conversation.

I opened a support ticket at 01:16. GreenCloud replied at 01:19 and began investigating the provider-side source.

The unwanted multicast traffic stopped after they investigated the issue, so the underlying problem was fixed. The bandwidth that had already been recorded in the control panel was not removed afterward.

The initial technical response came in roughly three minutes, and the unwanted traffic was subsequently stopped.

Not recommended · Updated October 22, 2026

Servitro — Frankfurt, Germany

I purchased a VPS from Servitro to diversify my hosting across multiple providers. Unfortunately, it has become one of the worst VPS experiences I've had.

I brought up a Frankfurt VPS named Wurstcase for DN42, Yggdrasil, WireGuard, and other networking experiments. Initial network latency from Pennsylvania to Frankfurt was genuinely good: my first IPv6 tests were around 90–95 ms, with no packet loss in the small samples I ran.

Unfortunately, the actual VPS performance later became severely degraded by persistent CPU steal time. Repeated mpstat 1 10 samples collected across the evening, overnight, and the following morning showed sustained %steal averages around 14–24%, with individual one-second samples reaching as high as 74%.

Guest CPU usage itself remained relatively low, and %iowait was effectively zero in the samples I collected. The combination of low guest workload and high CPU steal strongly indicated host-side CPU contention rather than a workload problem inside my VM.

I opened support ticket #FHO-567811 on October 14, 2026 at 22:54, including multiple mpstat captures demonstrating the problem.

I followed up on October 15 after the issue persisted for more than twelve hours, providing additional measurements showing average CPU steal around 20–24% and individual spikes reaching 74%. I requested an investigation of the host node and migration if necessary.

By October 16 at 14:25, the ticket had been open for approximately 39 hours and 31 minutes. In the ticket history I reviewed, every visible message during that period was one of my own updates. I had not received a visible response from support.

During that time, performance deteriorated enough that establishing an SSH session became difficult. I requested either migration to a healthy host or cancellation with an appropriate refund or credit.

October 19, 2026 · Incident update

Update: It keeps getting worse

Following the original CPU steal complaints, Servitro migrated my VPS to another host node. CPU steal appeared to improve substantially after the migration. Unfortunately, the migration introduced new problems.

First, SSH access failed following the migration, requiring troubleshooting through the VNC console. After restoring access, I discovered that public IPv6 connectivity was completely broken.

I investigated and captured network traffic showing that the configured IPv6 gateway was not responding to Neighbor Discovery requests. IPv4 was still working at that point, so I restored an affected DN42 BGP peering session by switching its WireGuard transport to IPv4.

I reported the IPv6 failure to Servitro support.

Then the situation got substantially worse.

Later on October 19, the VPS stopped responding over public IPv4 as well. The VNC console also stopped connecting, leaving me unable to access the server through either the network or the provider's console.

The control panel reported 0% CPU utilization, unavailable memory information, and zero network traffic. Although the service continued to be listed as Active, I could not determine whether the virtual machine was running.

At 12:10 PM on October 19, I attempted to start the VPS using the control panel's Boot function. The operation was cancelled after approximately 20 seconds with this message:

The system failed to respond to the action request. Further attempts are likely to be cancelled until the system is responding normally. Try again in five minutes.

This suggests a possible problem with the host or VPS management infrastructure. I cannot independently confirm the cause without access to the hypervisor.

At the time of this October 19 update, the VPS was still inaccessible and I had no explanation for the outage. Servitro subsequently confirmed that the host node had been shut down because its CPU temperature reached a critical level.

I originally purchased this VPS to diversify my infrastructure across independent hosting providers and improve redundancy. Instead, I have spent days troubleshooting a service that was supposed to provide an additional reliable network location.

October 20–22, 2026 · Recovery update

Back online, with another IPv6 problem to solve

On October 20, Servitro restored Wurstcase and confirmed my data was intact. The VPS had been down for over 20 hours, substantially longer than their original estimate. CPU steal had improved on the new host, but the networking problems were not over.

The IPv6 gateway was initially still unreachable. Servitro acknowledged that problem was on their side and repaired it. Afterward, IPv6 connections to Google and Cloudflare worked, including HTTPS. But Wurstcase and my Clanker VPS still could not reach each other over public IPv6 in either direction.

I provided more tests and packet captures showing traffic leaving Wurstcase and replies failing to reach Clanker. Servitro eventually confirmed that their IPv6 range was unreachable from some external networks and said they had escalated the issue to their upstream provider. A control-panel reboot they recommended was delayed in the queue before eventually completing; it did not solve the remaining routing issue.

On October 22, Servitro reported that its upstream provider had fixed the IPv6 routing. My subsequent tests showed working external IPv6 connectivity and restored connectivity between Wurstcase and Clanker. The VPS and tested IPv6 paths are working again as of this update.

I appreciate the technical fixes, but the communication often felt disconnected from the ticket history. I spent considerable time gathering diagnostics, yet several replies seemed to overlook them. After I explicitly reported that IPv6 was working and said the ticket could be closed, the next response still gave generic instructions for restarting the VPS if IPv6 wasn't working.

October 2026 · Ticket #FHO-567811

My conclusion

Cheap VPS hosting inevitably involves shared resources, and a single overloaded host would not by itself make me write off a provider. Infrastructure problems happen.

What changed my opinion was the combination of severe, persistent host contention, inadequate communication, and further failures following the host migration. What started as a performance issue progressed to broken IPv6 connectivity and eventually a completely inaccessible VPS. These problems have since been repaired, but resolving them involved days of troubleshooting and repeated follow-ups.

I have encountered provider-side networking problems with other inexpensive VPS providers where support acknowledged the issue within minutes and began investigating. Problems are inevitable; how a provider responds is a major part of the service.

I understand that budget VPS hosting does not include enterprise-level support. However, expecting timely acknowledgment of an unusable service and meaningful updates during a complete outage is not unreasonable.

Servitro offered compensation for the outage. I requested a three-month service extension on October 20 and followed up on October 22. They declined the extension but added $2 in account credit toward my next invoice. Since this is a $20/year VPS, that is 10% of the annual price. I think it was a fair goodwill gesture, and I thanked them for it.

I will not be renewing my service with Servitro. The low price simply is not worth the instability, repeated troubleshooting, and lack of timely communication.

Servitro: Managed hosting, except the customer manages the incident response. 💀

Based on this experience, I do not recommend Servitro for workloads where predictable performance, availability, or timely support matters.
