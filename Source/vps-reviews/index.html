My experience with GreenCloud

I currently use GreenCloud VPS for multiple public Linux VPS nodes, including Clanker and ScopeCreep.

These are not idle test machines. I use them for Docker services, monitoring, web applications, DN42 routing, WireGuard, DNS, small Internet-protocol servers, and networking experiments.

GreenCloud has been a good fit for the kind of inexpensive Linux infrastructure I run: enough resources to do useful work, cheap enough that keeping multiple locations online is practical, and support that has responded quickly when I have hit provider-side network problems.

Support experience

I have had a couple of network problems worth mentioning. I think those incidents are more useful in a review than pretending nothing has ever gone wrong, because they show what support does when the infrastructure is actually broken.

August 2026

IPv6 provisioning issue

After enabling IPv6 on one VPS, Neighbor Discovery to the supplied gateway worked and outbound packets left the VM, but nothing came back. Packet captures indicated that the problem was upstream of the guest.

I opened a ticket at 07:30. Support replied at 07:32, investigated the VPS, and at 07:48 asked me to retry after making a change. I confirmed IPv6 was working at 07:54.

About 24 minutes from opening the ticket to confirmed working IPv6.

September 2026

Unsolicited IPv6 multicast traffic

On another VPS I noticed inbound bandwidth increasing even though my applications were not receiving that amount of traffic.

A packet capture showed unsolicited ICMPv6 / MLDv2 multicast listener reports from another device on the provider-side network. During a 300-second capture I measured roughly 72,007 frames, about 107 MB, or approximately 2.85 Mbps of sustained inbound traffic from that conversation.

I opened a support ticket at 01:16. GreenCloud replied at 01:19 and began investigating the provider-side source.

The unwanted multicast traffic stopped after they investigated the issue, so the underlying problem was fixed. The bandwidth that had already been recorded in the control panel was not removed afterward.

The initial technical response came in roughly three minutes, and the unwanted traffic was subsequently stopped.

Not recommended · October 2026

Servitro — Frankfurt, Germany

My experience with Servitro started out promising and then deteriorated badly enough that I can no longer recommend the service.

I brought up a Frankfurt VPS named Wurstcase for DN42, Yggdrasil, WireGuard, and other networking experiments. Initial network latency from Pennsylvania to Frankfurt was genuinely good: my first IPv6 tests were around 90–95 ms with no packet loss in the small samples I ran.

Unfortunately, the actual VPS performance later became severely degraded by persistent CPU steal time. Repeated mpstat 1 10 samples taken across the evening, overnight, and the following morning showed sustained %steal averages around 14–24%, with individual one-second samples reaching as high as 74%.

Guest CPU usage itself remained relatively low and %iowait was effectively zero in the samples I collected. The combination of low guest workload and high CPU steal strongly indicated host-side CPU contention rather than a workload problem inside my VM.

I opened support ticket #FHO-567811 on October 14, 2026 at 22:54 and included multiple mpstat captures showing the problem.

I followed up on October 15 after the issue persisted for more than twelve hours, providing additional measurements showing average CPU steal around 20–24% and individual spikes as high as 74%. I specifically asked Servitro to investigate the host node and migrate the VM if necessary.

By October 16 at 14:25, the ticket had been open for approximately 39 hours and 31 minutes. In the ticket history I reviewed, every visible message during that period was one of my own updates; I had not received a visible response from Servitro support.

During that time the VPS continued to deteriorate. By my final update, performance had become poor enough that I was having difficulty even establishing an SSH session long enough to collect additional diagnostics.

At that point I asked Servitro to either migrate the VM to a healthy host immediately or confirm that I could cancel the service and receive an appropriate refund or credit.

October 2026 · Ticket #FHO-567811

My conclusion

Cheap VPS hosting inevitably involves shared resources, and a single overloaded host would not by itself make me write off a provider. Infrastructure problems happen.

What changed my opinion was the combination of severe, persistent host contention and the lack of a visible support response for roughly forty hours after I supplied detailed diagnostic information.

That distinction matters to me. I have encountered provider-side networking problems with other inexpensive VPS providers where support acknowledged the issue within minutes and began investigating it. Problems are inevitable; how a provider responds to them is a major part of the service.

Based on this experience, I do not recommend Servitro for workloads where predictable performance or timely support matters.
