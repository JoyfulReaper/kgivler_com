./support_experience.sh

I have had a couple of network problems worth mentioning. I actually consider these useful examples because they show what support does when something is not working.

IPv6 provisioning issue — August 2026

After enabling IPv6 on one VPS, Neighbor Discovery to the supplied gateway worked and outbound packets left the VM, but nothing came back. Packet captures indicated that the problem was upstream of the guest.

I opened a ticket at 07:30. Support replied at 07:32, investigated the VPS, and at 07:48 asked me to retry after making a change. I confirmed IPv6 was working at 07:54.

That was about 24 minutes from opening the ticket to confirmed working IPv6.

Unsolicited IPv6 multicast traffic — September 2026

On another VPS I noticed inbound bandwidth increasing even though my applications were not receiving that amount of traffic.

A packet capture showed unsolicited ICMPv6 / MLDv2 multicast listener reports from another device on the provider-side network. During a 300-second capture I measured roughly 72,007 frames, about 107 MB, or approximately 2.85 Mbps of sustained inbound traffic from that conversation.

I opened a support ticket at 01:16. GreenCloud replied at 01:19 saying they were investigating the source and would ensure the VPS was not charged for those packets.

Whatever the underlying switch or multicast-isolation problem was, I appreciated getting a technical response in roughly three minutes rather than spending hours trying to convince first-line support that multicast traffic exists.

./good_and_bad.sh

What I like

  • Cheap enough for hobby and small-service infrastructure
  • KVM VPSes work well for normal Linux workloads
  • IPv6 is available
  • Multiple locations make multi-node experiments practical
  • Support has responded quickly to the problems I have reported
  • I can run normal Docker/networking workloads without needing a special platform

Things to know

  • I have encountered provider-side IPv6/networking issues
  • These are inexpensive VPSes, not managed enterprise infrastructure
  • I monitor CPU, memory, bandwidth, and service health myself
  • I would still design around backups and failure rather than trusting any single VPS
  • Plan availability and pricing change, so check the current offer before buying

./who_is_it_for.sh

I think GreenCloud makes the most sense if you are comfortable administering your own Linux server and want inexpensive compute for small applications, personal infrastructure, Docker workloads, networking experiments, or geographically separate nodes.

If you need a fully managed environment where somebody else owns the operating system, application stack, backups, and architecture, that is a different product category.
